## Where things stand

The hosted Supabase project exists and its region is pinned. **Nothing has been applied to the hosted database yet** — that is the founder's next step and it is the last irreversible one.

The security boundary is real and probed: the private `app` schema is absent from PostgREST's exposed-schemas list, so base tables are not addressable over HTTP whatever their grants say. RLS is the second line, not the boundary. The public surface is three projection tables with frozen column lists and capped RPCs.

**Two decisions were reversed, and they are two different sets. Do not conflate them.**

*Reversed by the kickoff itself, before any code* — marked `[REVERSED]` in `Sprint Kickoffs/sprint-kickoff-bedspace-v1-2026-09-08.md`: **F3**, freshness cannot come from the device clock, because a cheap Android three hours slow renders four-hour-old data green; **A1**, public reads come from a static snapshot rather than Realtime, because free-tier Realtime refuses connections past 200 concurrent and a dropped WebSocket looks open to JavaScript; and the **24-hour staleness ceiling**, where beyond 24h the count is suppressed rather than badged grey.

*Reversed during these sessions* — three:

- **`blood_status` is cut from Sprint 1 entirely.** Four exchanges hardened it — gating rules, three clinical conditions, a nullable timestamp — before anyone asked whether it should ship at all. It failed the standing test: nothing said what it means, who maintains it, or how often. Same call as the hospital accuracy score. The display-only verdict and its conditions are on file for whenever it does ship, and the column must then be `app.tri_state` and **not** a new type, so it inherits the duty-flag guards instead of escaping them.
- **Region `af-south-1` → `eu-west-1`.** Not a change of mind: **Supabase has no African region.** The original pin cited something that does not exist. Two claims in A4 were struck — "keeps data on the continent" was false, and the NDPA s.41 leg never followed from the geographic claim anyway, because s.41 turns on Nigeria versus not-Nigeria and Cape Town was as much a cross-border transfer as Dublin. Do not read the correction as losing a protection; there was none.
- **Ward-level identity supersedes A5.** No individual accounts. An auth account represents a ward, or a facility for admin, never a natural person. `actor_identity_map`, the severing design, the retention-period decision, `staff_contact` and the `REFERRER` role all stop existing. The audit log carries no identity-bearing column and **no `detail jsonb`** — a column-list guard is blind to `detail->>'ip'`, so keeping it would have made the guard vacuous against the exact attack it names.

### Ward categories — ten, and the kickoff never enumerated them

`A_AND_E`, `ICU_ADULT`, `ICU_PAEDIATRIC`, `MEDICAL_ADULT`, `PAEDIATRIC`, `THEATRE`, `SURGICAL`, `MATERNITY`, `NICU`, `SCBU`.

The kickoff states "all eight categories" as a Gate 3 contract and names `paediatrician closes NICU/SCBU` as a duty-flag rule, but **lists no values**. The set was chosen at implementation time, and an audit before the window closed found two defects in that choice.

**`ICU` is split by age** because ventilator circuits, tube sizes, drug dosing and nursing competency differ. A dispatcher routing a child to a facility showing free ICU beds that are adult beds has been handed a false positive — the same defect the duty-cover gate exists to prevent. `ICU` was deliberately *not* kept to mean adult: a label answering a narrower question than the reader asked is the defect being removed.

**`PAEDIATRIC` was added and `GENERAL_MEDICAL` became `MEDICAL_ADULT`**, because there was no tile at all for a general paediatric admission, which is commoner than a paediatric ICU one. It is not named `MEDICAL_PAEDIATRIC`: most children's wards take medical **and** post-surgical children in one unit, so the narrower name would be the same defect in the other direction. The asymmetry with `MEDICAL_ADULT` is correct because the reality is asymmetric.

`THEATRE`, `A_AND_E`, `SURGICAL` and `MATERNITY` are **deliberately not split** — usually one unit taking both adults and children. The male/female medical split stays closed, decided on a different axis (sex, not age), so splitting on age does not reopen it.

**Recorded residual:** if `PAEDIATRIC` absorbs post-operative children then `SURGICAL` is now *de facto* adult — the same implicit narrowing, one value out. This cannot be chased to completion by naming, because where wards divide by age varies by facility. It is clinician question 4.

The paediatrician flag now gates four categories (`NICU`, `SCBU`, `PAEDIATRIC`, `ICU_PAEDIATRIC`), flagged in both derivation sites as **a default pending clinician confirmation**. The flag itself is not split: most Nigerian tier-2 facilities have no neonatologist, so a separate neonatal flag would read `UNKNOWN` or `NO` permanently — a real distinction nobody keeps honest.

### The residue — "no personal data" is not zero, and one half is unmitigated

Ward-level identity leaves no natural person in the **operational record**, and that is what made four of the five original lawyer questions fall away. **It was never zero.** Two residues survive by design.

**Residue A — the login address. Not yet mitigated.** A magic link has to be sent somewhere, and that address lives in Supabase `auth.users` and in the email provider's delivery logs. A genuine role address — `maternity.ward@facility`, or the ward duty handset — identifies a ward and is not personal data. A nurse's personal Gmail is. **We cannot tell which we have been given**, there is no technical check that distinguishes them, and there will not be one.

The mitigation is contractual: **the facility agreement must state that supplied addresses are role addresses rather than individual ones, and that the facility is responsible for that.** It puts the obligation where the knowledge is. **That obligation currently exists nowhere** — not in a drafted agreement, not in an onboarding step, not in the schema. Until it is written, *"we hold no personal data"* is aspirational rather than true — and it is the premise the ward-identity decision, the retention answer and the four dropped lawyer questions all rest on.

**Residue B — the invited facility contact. Built, and correctly named.** One human per facility, business-contact data on a **contract** basis, in its own table, no append-only trigger, deletable on request. Confirmed against the live catalogue rather than assumed: `app.facility_contact` holds `full_name`, `job_title`, `email` and `mobile_e164`, and `agreement_accepted_at` was built. Two properties are load-bearing: it is named for a facility **agreement** and not a privacy notice, because the basis is contract and not consent, and renaming it would invite a future reader to reason from the wrong lawful basis; and it is deliberately NULLable, because the facility whose agreement is unaccepted is the one to chase and `NOT NULL` would force a placeholder timestamp.

### What the two probes establish — and what they do not

**Merge-blocking.** A/B/A on PR #1 with branch-protection held constant: six checks green → `CLEAN`, two red → `BLOCKED`, six green → `CLEAN`. All six had reported in every state, so this is not the checks-pending case, and the return to `CLEAN` rules out a one-way transition. **What it does not establish:** this is GitHub's *computed* status plus independently verified `enforce_admins`. ### One control lapsed — and it is one the CTO review set

**CTO condition (1) of the ward-identity memo was not actually enforced for a period.** The guard for *no identity-bearing column on `app.audit_log`* branched on whether `grep` found a forbidden name — and `grep` returns 0 for match, 1 for no match, and **2 for could-not-run**. Written as an `if`, a check that failed to execute reported "no forbidden column found". **The guard was failing open**, which is precisely the outcome it exists to prevent.

It was found and fixed on 2026-09-10, and the shape is now banned mechanically rather than remembered. Two things worth the boardroom's attention: the schema itself was always correct, so **no identity-bearing column ever existed** — what lapsed was the assurance, not the property. And the condition was set in a memo, implemented as a guard, and the guard silently stopped reaching; that is a governance failure mode, not a bash one, and it is the fifth instance this fortnight of *a mechanism present and not reaching*. ## Fundamental — carries forward

> **Everything must flow. The system must work end to end. Do not build pretty standalone pieces that do not connect.**

**The bundle order is layer-shaped** — the whole write path, then the whole read path — **and that is precisely the shape that produces disconnected parts.** The dependencies are real, so the sequence is right. What has to change is the depth taken in each pass.

1. **Cut a thin vertical slice before widening anything.** Bundle 2, plus the minimum of 3, plus the minimum of 4: one ward account signs in, publishes one bed count for one category, and that number reaches the public dashboard through the projection and the snapshot. Ugly, narrow, **connected**. Then widen. A mismatch between write and read is discovered on day two rather than after both are finished.
2. **The golden-path E2E is the spine, not Bundle 6's deliverable.** Write it early and let it be red. Each bundle turns more of it green, and it cannot go green until the pieces genuinely connect. Left until Bundle 6 it becomes an acceptance test for work already declared done, which is when integration defects cost most.
3. **Every cross-component contract has one derivation site.** The snapshot's shape is a contract between the generator and the dashboard; if both restate it they drift while both stay green. One checked-in fixture, both sides import it, neither restates it.
4. **One freshness anchor, enforced rather than remembered.** Freshness is derived client-side against **server** time — F3, already reversed once. A compliance guard should be added banning direct clock access in display modules, so every future freshness-rendering field inherits the protection instead of re-litigating it. *(Not built — a Bundle 4 instruction, not a control that exists.)*
5. **Nothing merges that is not reachable from the golden path.** If a feature cannot be reached by a user walking the dashboard from the front, it is a standalone thing however well it is tested.

**Two things already decided — do not rebuild them.** Bundle 2 is **ward-scoped**: no individual accounts, no `actor_identity_map`, no `REFERRER` role, no identity-bearing column and no `detail jsonb`. And Bundle 6's outcome loop is **ward-to-ward**: a referring clinician closing the loop reintroduces an individual unless it is ward-scoped from the start. **That is the single place the ward-identity decision gets undone by accident**, and it will look like a natural thing to do at the time.

**And the standing rule.** Any failure must be foundationally resolved: fix the root cause, understand what a fix touches before calling it done, and resolve it in the same pass rather than filing it.

### A1. The CI intermittent — closed, and the defect under it

**Status: closed 2026-09-10, on the stop rule's *make the job deterministic*
branch — not on a green run, and not on a diagnosis of the transient.**

**Read this distinction before reopening anything.** Two different things were
wrong, and only one of them is now understood:

| | Established? |
|---|---|
| **Why a false violation could be reported at all** | **Yes — named, fixed, and now guarded.** |
| **Which transient event triggered it on 2026-09-10** | **No.** It can no longer produce a false verdict, so it is no longer load-bearing. |

Closing on the first without pretending to the second is the honest reading. A
recurrence of the *shape* — see the stop rule at the end — would still be a
finding.

#### What happened

`tests/compliance/lint_migration_header.test.ts > a well-formed plant is
accepted` failed on GitHub Actions at commit `91c7108` (run `34410413362`),
reporting only `AssertionError: expected 1 to be +0`. The lint had exited 1,
which in `scripts/lint_migration_header.sh` can happen **only** when a `FAIL:`
line was printed — so a check genuinely rejected a file. Which file, and which
check, is unknown: the assertion discarded the lint's stdout.

The commit under test added **one file — this document, then named
`handoff-2026-09-09.md` — 209 lines**,
and changed nothing else. The lint's inputs were therefore byte-identical to
commit `f03f066`, where the same job passed.

#### The decisive experiment

The job was re-run on the **identical commit, with no changes**. It passed, and
all six required checks went green.

Same commit, same inputs, opposite outcomes. The failure is **nondeterministic**.
That is a finding, not a diagnosis.

#### Hypotheses excluded, each by a probe rather than by argument

#### The date-boundary premise, corrected

The date hypothesis arrived with the claim that this failed at a UTC date
boundary. **It did not.**

| Run | Created (UTC) | Result |
|---|---|---|
| `34408423408` (`f03f066`) | `2026-09-09T21:42:50Z` | pass |

#### What was changed, and what it is not

Both legs of the trio that assert **acceptance** now attach the lint's stdout to
the assertion, and the ACCEPT leg additionally prints the scratch directory
listing and the exact bytes written for `900_valid.sql`. That separates the three
states `expected 1 to be +0` cannot: a missing file, a wrong file, or a genuinely
nondeterministic lint.

**This was instrumentation, not a fix** — and it worked on its first outing. The
very next CI failure, on 2026-09-10, reported:

```
FAIL: 006_gate_function.sql: no '-- ===' banner block at the top of the file
```

#### What that one line settled

It named the **`real corpus is accepted`** leg — which reads `REPO_ROOT`
directly, with no scratch tree, no copy and no plant. So every hypothesis about
the plant, the copy and the temp directory died at once.

And the claim was impossible on its face. `database/migrations/006_gate_function.sql`
begins with the byte `-` and carries a **58-line** banner. The banner was fine.
**A check reported a violation it had not found.**

That leaves exactly one shape of explanation: `grep` returned non-zero for a
reason other than *no match*.

#### The defect, which is independent of whatever triggered it

**`grep` has three exit codes and the lint branched on two.**

| Exit | Means | How the lint read it |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | match | fine |
| 1 | no match | a finding |
| **2** | **could not run** — unreadable input, resource failure, a fork that did not happen | **also "a finding"** |

Written as `check || fail`, exit 2 became **a violation that does not exist**.
Written as `if check; then`, exit 2 became **"clean"** — and that form was live in
`scripts/lint_audit_log_columns.sh`, the guard for CTO condition (1): *no
identity-bearing column on `app.audit_log`*. **That guard was failing open.** A
grep that failed to run would have reported no forbidden column, which is
precisely the outcome the guard exists to prevent.

This is the fourth-instance pattern from `.claude/rules/test-conventions.md` §8
again, one layer down: **a check reporting a verdict it did not reach.**

#### The fix — made deterministic, not tuned

The three banner checks were `printf '%s\n' "$banner" | grep -q … || fail`. They
are now pure-bash `case` matching: **no fork, no pipe, and no third exit code to
misread.** bash 3.2 compatible. The one check that must genuinely read a file
keeps `grep`, with its exit codes separated explicitly — 1 is a finding, anything
else is fatal and loud.

Three scripts carried the shape and all three are fixed:
`lint_migration_header.sh`, `lint_audit_log_columns.sh`, `lint_from_allowlist.sh`.

**Fork pressure fell as a side effect**, which matters if the transient was
resource-related: the header lint forked roughly nine processes per migration
across fourteen migrations on every invocation, with the compliance suite
invoking the lints across ten concurrent vitest workers. Three of those forks per
file are now gone.

**And the shape is now banned rather than remembered.**
`scripts/lint_no_piped_grep_q.sh` rejects any lint script that branches on
`<writer> | grep -q`, with plants for both directions — the false-violation form
and the fail-open form — plus a leg proving a commented-out occurrence is not a
violation, since every one of these scripts documents the banned shape in its own
header.

**Trigger B (2026-09-24) is discharged early**: the diagnostic work happened on
2026-09-10 rather than at the backstop, which is what the date existed to force.

**Trigger A stays live, and is not scoped to this file.** It is kept below
verbatim, because the shape it names is a property of the test suite rather than
of an item that is now closed. Its bar is unchanged: a compliance leg asserting
acceptance goes red in CI and green locally on the same commit, with the local
green **demonstrated on the identical commit, not presumed**.

##### Trigger A — recurrence. No date attached.

The instrumentation fires whenever it fires; it needs no calendar. On a
recurrence, exactly one of these two, and nothing in between:

- **A Standard O flaky carve-out**, with everything that standard demands and
  nothing less: three fresh-database full-suite runs showing pass and fail with
  no code change; visible quarantine that keeps the test running and reported
  rather than a bare skip; a tracked ticket with a determinism plan; and its own
  sunset date.
- **Or the job is made deterministic**, and this item closes.

**Recurrence is defined by SHAPE, not by file.** The signature is: *a compliance
guard leg that asserts acceptance — ACCEPT or ANTI-VACUITY — goes red in CI and
green locally on the same commit.* Defining it by filename would assume the cause
is file-specific, which is precisely what nobody knows.

**The precondition that keeps this from becoming a bucket:** local-green on the
**identical commit** must be *demonstrated, not presumed*, before a red is
attributed to this item. Without that, any CI red gets swept in on the assumption
that it would pass locally — and then item 9 is the growing quarantine list
Standard O warns about, which is the failure mode resurfacing rather than being
managed.

### A2. Things that will look wrong and are not

- **`supabase/migrations/` is empty and `[db.migrations] enabled = false`** —
  deliberate. Two runners over one schema corrupts it. `supabase db reset` does
  **not** restore this schema; use `npm run db:reset`.
- **`auto_expose_new_tables = true`** in `supabase/config.toml` — deliberately at
  the cloud default. Hardening it locally would make the RLS negative suite pass
