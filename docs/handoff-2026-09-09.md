## 3. Ward categories — why ten, and what is deliberately not split

`A_AND_E`, `ICU_ADULT`, `ICU_PAEDIATRIC`, `MEDICAL_ADULT`, `PAEDIATRIC`,
`THEATRE`, `SURGICAL`, `MATERNITY`, `NICU`, `SCBU`.

**The kickoff never enumerated these.** It states "all eight categories" as a
Gate 3 contract and names `paediatrician closes NICU/SCBU` as a duty-flag rule,
and lists no values. The set was chosen at implementation time; an audit before
the window closed found two defects in that choice.

- **`ICU` split by age.** Ventilator circuits, tube sizes, drug dosing and nursing
  competency differ. A dispatcher routing a child to a facility showing free ICU
  beds that are adult beds has been handed a false positive — the same defect the
  duty-cover gate exists to prevent. `ICU` was **not** kept to mean adult: a label
  answering a narrower question than the reader asked is the defect being removed.
- **`PAEDIATRIC` added, `GENERAL_MEDICAL` → `MEDICAL_ADULT`.** There was no tile
  at all for a general paediatric admission, which is commoner than a paediatric
  ICU one. Not named `MEDICAL_PAEDIATRIC`: most children's wards take medical
  **and** post-surgical children in one unit, so the narrower name is the same
  defect in the other direction. The asymmetry with `MEDICAL_ADULT` is correct
  because the reality is asymmetric.

**Not split, deliberately:** `THEATRE`, `A_AND_E`, `SURGICAL`, `MATERNITY` are
usually one unit taking both adults and children.

**Recorded residual:** if `PAEDIATRIC` absorbs post-operative children then
`SURGICAL` is now *de facto* adult — the same implicit narrowing one value out.
This cannot be chased to completion by naming, because where wards divide by age
varies by facility. It is clinician question 4.

The male/female medical split stays closed — decided on a **different axis**
(sex, not age), so splitting on age does not reopen it.

**The paediatrician flag now gates four categories** (`NICU`, `SCBU`,
`PAEDIATRIC`, `ICU_PAEDIATRIC`), flagged in both derivation sites as a **default
pending clinician confirmation**. It is transcription of an existing rule, not a
new one. The flag itself is **not** split: most Nigerian tier-2 facilities have no
neonatologist, so a separate neonatal flag would read `UNKNOWN` or `NO`
permanently — a real distinction nobody keeps honest.

### Stop rule — two triggers, doing two different jobs

The date and the recurrence path answer different questions. Collapsing them into
one is what turns a sunset review into a deferral, so they are kept apart.

#### Trigger A — recurrence. No date attached.

The instrumentation fires whenever it fires; it needs no calendar. On a
recurrence, exactly one of these two, and nothing in between:

- **A Standard O flaky carve-out**, with everything that standard demands and
  nothing less: three fresh-database full-suite runs showing pass and fail with
  no code change; visible quarantine that keeps the test running and reported
  rather than a bare skip; a tracked ticket with a determinism plan; and its own
  sunset date.
- **Or the job is made deterministic**, and this item closes.

**Recurrence is defined by SHAPE, not by file.** The signature is: *a compliance
guard leg that asserts acceptance — ACCEPT or ANTI-VACUITY — goes red in CI and
green locally on the same commit.* Defining it by filename would assume the cause
is file-specific, which is precisely what nobody knows.

**The precondition that keeps this from becoming a bucket:** local-green on the
**identical commit** must be *demonstrated, not presumed*, before a red is
attributed to this item. Without that, any CI red gets swept in on the assumption
that it would pass locally — and then item 9 is the growing quarantine list
Standard O warns about, which is the failure mode resurfacing rather than being
managed.

#### Trigger B — 2026-09-24. A commitment to do the work.

**On that date the diagnostic work happens, whether or not it has recurred.** The
date does not exist to ask "is it still failing?" — a review with only that
question has nothing to decide, and becomes the deferral it was meant to prevent.

Fourteen days, because the binding constraint is **context decay, not run
volume**. The excluded hypotheses in the table above, and the probes that
excluded them, are live understanding right now; in ninety days this section is a
paragraph someone skims on the way to something else.

Run volume was the argument for a longer date, and it does not survive this

## 9. The residue — why "no personal data" is not zero

§2 records that ward-level identity leaves no natural person in the system. That
is true of the **operational record**, and it is what made four of the five
original lawyer questions fall away. It was never *zero*, and until now neither
residue was written down anywhere in this document.

### Residue A — the login address. **This one is not yet mitigated.**

A magic link has to be sent somewhere. That address lives in Supabase
`auth.users` and in the email provider's delivery logs.

- A genuine **role address** — `maternity.ward@facility`, or the ward duty
  handset — identifies a ward, not a person, and is not personal data.
- A nurse's personal Gmail **is** personal data.
- **We cannot tell which we have been given.** There is no technical check that
  distinguishes them, and there will not be one.

The mitigation is contractual, not technical: **the facility agreement states
that supplied addresses are role addresses rather than individual ones, and that
the facility is responsible for that.** It puts the obligation where the
knowledge is.

**That obligation currently exists nowhere** — not in a drafted agreement, not in
an onboarding step, not in the schema. Until it is written, *"we hold no personal
data"* is aspirational rather than true, and it is the premise that the
ward-identity decision, the retention answer and the four dropped lawyer
questions all rest on. It is §1 item 10, and it is the reason that row exists.

### Residue B — the invited facility contact. Built, and correctly named.

One human per facility. Business-contact data on a **contract** basis, in its own
table, with no append-only trigger, deletable on request. This is where the
single human per facility went when `app.staff_contact` was dropped.

**Confirmed against the live catalogue on 2026-09-10, not assumed.**
`app.facility_contact` holds `full_name`, `job_title`, `email` and `mobile_e164`
— that is the residue, concretely — and `agreement_accepted_at timestamptz` **was
built**, at
`database/migrations/003_app_facility_and_identity_tables.sql:328`.

Two properties of that column are load-bearing and must survive future tidying:

- **It is named for a facility agreement, not a privacy notice.** There is no
  consent basis here; the basis is contract. Renaming it to
  `privacy_notice_accepted_at` — or treating it as a consent record — would
  invite a future reader to reason from the wrong lawful basis.
- **It is deliberately NULLable.** An unaccepted agreement has to be
  representable, because the facility whose agreement is unaccepted is the one to
  chase. `NOT NULL` would force a placeholder timestamp, which is worse evidence
  than an honest absence.

### The one remaining lawyer question — non-blocking

Does a ward role address held in an auth table, carrying no name and no
individual link, sit outside the NDPA's personal-data definition on these facts?

**Ask it when the facility agreement is drafted, not before.** It is not blocking
for facility #1, and it is the only legal item still standing.

| Bundle | Title | Depends on |
|---|---|---|

| 3 | Ward write path | Bundle 2, for an account that can write |
| 4 | Public read path | Bundle 3, for something to read |
| 5 | Escalation and notification reliability | Bundles 3–4, for a state worth escalating |
| 6 | Outcome capture (golden-path tail) | Bundle 4, for a tile that was referred from |

### The founder's instruction, and how to actually satisfy it

> **Everything must flow. The system must work end to end. Do not build pretty
> standalone pieces that do not connect.**

**The bundle order is layer-shaped** — the whole write path, then the whole read
path — **and that is precisely the shape that produces disconnected parts.** The
dependencies above are real, so the *sequence* is right. What has to change is
the **depth taken in each pass.**

**1. Cut a thin vertical slice before widening anything.** Bundle 2, plus the
minimum of 3, plus the minimum of 4: one ward account signs in, publishes one bed
count for one category, and that number appears on the public dashboard through
the projection and the snapshot. Ugly, narrow, **connected**. *Then* widen 3 to
all categories, reason codes and concurrency, and widen 4 to filtering, distance
sort and the rollup. A mismatch between write and read is then discovered on day
two rather than after both are finished.

**2. The golden-path E2E is the spine, not Bundle 6's deliverable.** Write it
early and **let it be red**. Each bundle turns more of it green, and it cannot go
green until the pieces genuinely connect — the system-level version of the
anti-vacuity leg. Left until Bundle 6, it becomes an acceptance test for work
already declared done, which is when integration defects cost the most.

**3. Every cross-component contract has one derivation site.** The snapshot's
shape is a contract between the generator and the dashboard. If both restate it
they drift while both stay green — §7 of `.claude/rules/test-conventions.md`,
applied one layer out. One checked-in fixture, both sides import it, neither
restates it.

**4. One freshness anchor, enforced rather than remembered.** Freshness is
derived client-side against **server** time — F3, already reversed once because a
slow device clock renders stale data green. Every freshness derivation in the
client uses the same helper, and **a compliance guard should be added banning
direct clock access** (`Date.now()`, `new Date()` with no argument) in display
modules, so every future freshness-rendering field inherits the protection
instead of re-litigating it. *(Not built yet — this is a Bundle 4 instruction,
not a control that exists.)*

**5. Nothing merges that is not reachable from the golden path.** If a feature
cannot be reached by a user walking the dashboard from the front, it is a
standalone thing however well it is tested. Either wire it in, or do not build it
yet.

### Two things already decided — do not rebuild them

- **Bundle 2 is ward-scoped.** No individual accounts, no `actor_identity_map`,
  no `REFERRER` role, no identity-bearing column and no `detail jsonb` in the
  audit log. §2 carries the reasoning.
- **Bundle 6's outcome loop is ward-to-ward.** A referring clinician closing the
  loop reintroduces an individual unless it is scoped to a ward account from the
  start. **This is the single place the ward-identity decision gets undone by
  accident**, and it will look like a natural thing to do at the time.

**Fundamental, carried forward.** Any failure must be foundationally resolved:
fix the root cause, understand what a fix touches before calling it done, and
resolve it in the same pass rather than filing it.
